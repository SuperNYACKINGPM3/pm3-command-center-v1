# M.A.G.I.C. AI Framework™ + AI Pop-Up Shop™

A plain-language teaching framework and portable public learning experience for helping beginners work with AI safely, clearly, and productively.

## Central Teaching Idea

AI is a tool that responds to instructions, patterns, examples, context, and feedback. It does not possess human wisdom, conscience, or independent judgment.

**M.A.G.I.C.™** teaches five repeatable habits:

- **M — Master the Input**
- **A — Aim the Attention**
- **G — Govern the Strategy**
- **I — Inspect the Output**
- **C — Convert Results into Capability**

---

## 1. Master the Input

AI needs a clear key before it can open the right door. The key is the instruction: what is needed, why it is needed, who it is for, what information should be used, and what the finished result should look like.

**Weak instruction:**

> Help me with business.

**Strong instruction:**

> Give me three low-cost service-business ideas for one person in Fort Wayne. Each should cost under $100 to start and have a path to recurring revenue.

**Teaching formula:**

> Clear goal + useful context + constraints + desired format = stronger AI request

**Exercise:** Improve one vague prompt by adding a goal, audience, context, boundaries, and output format.

---

## 2. Aim the Attention

AI tends to follow the direction of the words, patterns, and examples placed in front of it. Tell it what to focus on, not only what to avoid.

**Weak instruction:**

> Do not make this confusing or too long.

**Strong instruction:**

> Explain this in plain language for a ninth-grade reader, using one example and no more than five short paragraphs.

**Teaching principle:** Positive specifications are usually more useful than vague prohibitions. Describe what good looks like, which details matter, what must remain unchanged, and which examples to follow.

**Guardrail:** Framing and repetition affect what people notice, request, practice, and evaluate, but truth still requires evidence.

---

## 3. Govern the Strategy

Do not use one model, prompt, or workflow for every problem. AI is one tool in the toolbox. The operator chooses when to use it, how to use it, and when not to trust it.

### Example: Business Decision

1. Use AI to brainstorm options.
2. Use reliable sources to verify facts.
3. Use a spreadsheet to calculate costs.
4. Use human judgment to make the decision.

**Strategy rule:**

> Right problem → right tool → right method → verified result

---

## 4. Inspect the Output

### Check. Challenge. Confirm.

- **Check:** Does the response answer the actual question?
- **Challenge:** What assumptions, omissions, biases, or unsupported claims might be present?
- **Confirm:** Can the important facts be verified through evidence or an authoritative source?

AI can sound confident while being wrong. Never confuse fluent language with verified truth.

> **PM3 Doctrine:** AI generates. Wisdom Engine™ verifies. Human judgment decides.

---

## 5. Convert Results into Capability

AI should not be used only to consume answers. It should help people create skills, systems, and assets. AI may increase productive capacity, but economic value still requires a real problem, a useful solution, ownership, execution, and trust.

| Level | User Behavior | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Consume | Requests isolated answers | Temporary convenience |
| 2. Learn | Uses AI to understand and practice | Increased capability |
| 3. Produce | Creates useful work | Deliverable value |
| 4. Systemize | Builds repeatable workflows or IP | Reusable asset |

### Example: A Cook Using AI

1. Explain a cooking technique.
2. Organize shift notes.
3. Create a repeatable closing checklist.
4. Convert that checklist into a training product.

That is the movement from answer to asset.

---

## Complete M.A.G.I.C. Loop™

> Master the Input → Aim the Attention → Govern the Strategy → Inspect the Output → Convert Results into Capability → Learn, improve, repeat

**One-sentence version:** Tell AI clearly, focus it properly, use it strategically, verify its work, and turn the result into real human capability.

## Plain-Language AI Definition

Artificial intelligence is computer technology designed to recognize patterns and generate outputs from instructions, examples, and information. It can help people write, organize, compare, analyze, and create, but it does not automatically know what is true, wise, fair, safe, or best.

### What AI Does Well

- Generates possibilities
- Reorganizes information
- Explains concepts
- Drafts content
- Detects patterns
- Accelerates repeatable work

### What Humans Must Retain

- Purpose
- Values
- Verification
- Responsibility
- Final judgment

# AI Pop-Up Shop™

A portable, beginner-friendly AI learning and assistance experience that can operate in libraries, schools, churches, community centers, workforce programs, small-business events, and neighborhood spaces.

## Recommended Pilot Format

> **45-minute M.A.G.I.C. workshop + 45-minute open AI Help Bar**

Participants receive immediate education, practical assistance, one verified result, and one reusable asset.

## Six-Zone Visitor Experience

1. **Welcome and Intake** — Identify the visitor's goal, skill level, and privacy concerns.
2. **AI Basics Station** — Explain what AI is, what it can do, and what it cannot decide.
3. **Prompt Clinic** — Turn a vague request into a clear, usable instruction.
4. **Verification Bar** — Practice Check, Challenge, Confirm.
5. **Build Station** — Create one practical output such as a resume draft, checklist, study guide, business idea, or workflow.
6. **Capability Checkout** — Give the visitor a reusable prompt, next-step plan, and safe-use rule.

## Example Service Menu

- AI Basics Mini-Lesson
- Prompt Improvement Session
- Resume and Job-Search Support
- Small-Business Idea Clinic
- Digital Organization Help
- Study and Learning Support
- Workflow and Checklist Builder
- Family AI Safety Orientation
- Educator Resource Builder

## Privacy and Safety Controls

- Do not enter passwords, Social Security numbers, banking credentials, medical records, or confidential employer data into public AI tools.
- Use fictional or redacted examples during demonstrations.
- Explain when information may be stored by a tool.
- Obtain permission before using a participant's data.
- Verify consequential claims before use.
- Keep final decisions with the participant.
- Do not present the shop as a substitute for licensed medical, legal, financial, or mental-health professionals.
