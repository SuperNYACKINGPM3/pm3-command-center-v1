# PIAS-000 — PM3 Governed Intelligence Core Specification

**Status:** Working Draft — Evidence-Gated  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Owner:** Paul Mason III / PM3  
**Parent:** PM3 Intelligence Atlas™  
**Doctrine:** AI generates. Wisdom Engine™ verifies. Human judgment decides.

## 1. Purpose
PIAS-000 defines the minimum operating language for governed human-AI decision work inside PM3. It is intentionally narrow. It does not define a company, certification program, academy, platform, or research institute.

The specification exists to make live decisions observable, reviewable, and improvable while preserving human authority.

## 2. Core Rule
No consequential AI-assisted decision is treated as trustworthy merely because the output is fluent, plausible, or confident.

A decision must be evaluated according to consequence, evidence, relevant intelligence modules, and human authority.

## 3. Consequence Ladder

### C0 — Routine
Low-impact, reversible actions. AI may assist or execute within predefined bounds.

### C1 — Material
Meaningful operational or financial impact, but readily reversible. Human review is expected where uncertainty or external commitments exist.

### C2 — High Consequence
Material financial, legal, employment, safety, privacy, reputational, or strategic exposure. Full verification and explicit human approval are required.

### C3 — Irreversible / Critical
Actions that are difficult or impossible to reverse, create durable legal or ownership consequences, transfer major rights, expose sensitive information, or commit substantial capital. Strong evidence, documented rationale, and explicit human authority are mandatory.

**Rule:** Evidence and review requirements increase with consequence.

## 4. Intelligence Module Registry

### Control Layer
- Intent Intelligence™ — clarifies the real objective, constraints, stakeholders, and success criteria.
- Meta-Intelligence™ — selects which intelligence modules, evidence thresholds, sequence, and review level should be used.
- Wisdom Engine™ — verifies evidence, reasoning, risk, alignment, and readiness to proceed.

### Perception Layer
- Semantic Intelligence™ — resolves meaning in context.
- Definition Intelligence™ — establishes and governs canonical definitions.
- Pattern Intelligence™ — identifies recurring structures and behaviors.
- Network Intelligence™ — maps relationships, dependencies, and leverage points.
- Provenance Intelligence™ — tracks origin, authorship, ownership, lineage, and transformations.
- Context Intelligence™ — identifies surrounding conditions that affect interpretation or action.
- Signal Intelligence™ — separates decision-relevant information from noise.

### Evaluation Layer
- Emotional Intelligence™ — detects emotional influence on judgment.
- Constraint Intelligence™ — identifies the dominant bottleneck.
- Temporal Intelligence™ — evaluates timing, sequence, readiness, and windows.
- Incentive Intelligence™ — identifies behaviors rewarded by a system.
- Counterfactual Intelligence™ — compares alternative actions and scenarios.
- Reversibility Intelligence™ — evaluates whether and at what cost a decision can be undone.
- Risk Intelligence™ — identifies and evaluates downside, uncertainty, and mitigation.
- Ethical Intelligence™ — evaluates duties, rights, values, and human impact.
- Adversarial Intelligence™ — tests how a system or decision could fail, be exploited, manipulated, or bypassed.
- Probability Intelligence™ — calibrates likelihood, uncertainty, and confidence.

### Deployment Layer
- Allocation Intelligence™ — directs scarce resources such as money, time, attention, talent, compute, and agent capacity.
- Execution Intelligence™ — converts governed decisions into monitored action.
- Asset Intelligence™ — identifies and compounds durable financial, intellectual, operational, data, relationship, and real-estate value.
- Learning Intelligence™ — converts outcomes, errors, evidence, and feedback into future improvement.

## 5. Activation Principle
Not every module should run on every decision.

Meta-Intelligence™ should activate the minimum sufficient set of modules based on:
- objective,
- consequence,
- uncertainty,
- domain,
- reversibility,
- evidence availability,
- and required human authority.

Over-processing is itself a failure mode when it adds cost or delay without improving decision quality.

## 6. Evidence Principle
Important claims must be traceable to evidence appropriate to the consequence level.

Evidence records should distinguish:
- observed fact,
- source-backed fact,
- inference,
- assumption,
- estimate,
- forecast,
- and unresolved unknown.

Missing evidence must be logged rather than silently converted into certainty.

## 7. Human Authority Principle
AI may generate, analyze, rank, simulate, draft, summarize, and recommend.

AI does not inherit legal, financial, ethical, or organizational authority merely from technical capability.

**Capability ≠ permission. Permission ≠ authority. Authority ≠ accountability.**

The accountable human retains final authority for C2 and C3 decisions unless a separate, explicit governance policy lawfully delegates a narrower action.

## 8. Escalation Principle
The system must pause or escalate when:
- required evidence is missing,
- domain ambiguity changes the meaning of the decision,
- material conflicts exist between sources,
- confidence is not commensurate with consequence,
- the action exceeds delegated authority,
- or the decision is C2/C3 and human approval has not occurred.

## 9. Decision Record Requirement
Every decision admitted to the PM3 Decision Dataset should capture at minimum:
- Decision ID
- Date
- Project / source workflow
- Decision statement
- Intended outcome
- Consequence level
- Intelligence modules invoked
- Evidence used
- Assumptions / unknowns
- Human authority
- Action taken
- Outcome
- Outcome date
- Error or gap
- Revision
- Canonical lesson, if approved
- Status

## 10. Validation-First Rule
PIAS-000 is subordinate to live execution evidence.

Call Trap™ remains first in the execution queue under Validation-First. The Atlas must be refined through evidence from Call Trap™, the Diagnostic, and other approved live workflows; it must not displace them.

## 11. Expansion Gate
No expansion into certification, academy, institute, university track, large benchmark program, or standalone platform is authorized before the evidence gate is met.

Minimum gate:
- 50 logged decisions,
- meaningful outcome coverage,
- at least 10 documented revisions,
- visible module-use patterns,
- at least one case where the architecture materially improved a decision,
- and at least one case where the architecture added unnecessary friction.

## 12. Versioning Rule
PIAS-000 changes must be versioned. Definitions or rules should change because evidence, contradiction, implementation experience, or deliberate governance review warrants the change—not because a rule becomes inconvenient.

## 13. Current Research Question
Can a modular, consequence-sensitive, human-governed intelligence architecture improve decision reliability, explainability, auditability, and learning compared with ordinary unstructured AI assistance?
