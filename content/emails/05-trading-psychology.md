Hey {{first_name}},

The most dangerous trade isn't usually the first one. It's the one right after a loss.

That's when revenge trading shows up — sizing up to "make it back," skipping the checklist because
"you already know this one," or entering a trade purely to feel in control again.

None of that is a strategy problem. It's a state problem.

A few things that help:

- **Name the state before you trade.** Frustrated, anxious, and confident-after-a-win are all states
  that distort judgment in predictable ways.
- **Separate the decision to trade from the decision to feel better.** They are not the same
  decision, even though they feel identical in the moment.
- **Use a cooling-off rule.** A fixed pause after a loss — even ten minutes — breaks the reflex loop
  that leads to revenge trading.
- **Journal the reasoning, not just the result.** Patterns in your thinking are far more useful than
  patterns in your P&L.

This is exactly what the Weekly Review and Trade Journal inside the Command Center are built to
surface — not just what you traded, but what you were thinking when you did.
